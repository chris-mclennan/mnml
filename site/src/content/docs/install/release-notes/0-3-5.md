mnml 0.3.5 is the release where each integration shows one statusline
chip. Bitbucket PRs and Jira Work each fold their counters into that
one chip, and its hover says each number in words. The Segments menu
names the chips in words too, every chip's right-click menu has a
*Hide* row, the Bitbucket PRs chip no longer turns into the Pipelines
icon, and a chip's hover reads as one block that grows to its widest
line. Its Marketplace index names Jira and Bitbucket 0.2.4, which
carry the integration side of the change.

This release is 1 contributor over 17 commits since 0.3.4.

## Highlights

### One chip per integration

Bitbucket PRs and Jira Work used to spread their counts over two or
three statusline chips. Each now shows one.

The Bitbucket PRs chip reads your open pull requests first, then the
review threads on them, then the pull requests waiting on your review.
Each further count has its own small icon and is left off at zero. The
chip turns yellow while a review thread on yours waits on someone, and
orange while anything waits on your review. Opening the Bitbucket pane
keeps the thread count on the chip, since the pane counts threads from
the same shared cache.

The Jira Work chip carries the QA Actionable Now count beside the
assigned one.

A click on either chip opens its pane. Installing Jira and Bitbucket
0.2.4 takes the old second and third chips off the row. See
[On the statusline: one chip each](/docs/integrations#on-the-statusline-one-chip-each).

### The hover says the numbers in words

An integration chip's hover says each number in words, one line
apiece, such as *3 open pull requests of yours (2 still unapproved)*
and *2 waiting on your review*. The hover reads as one block: the
counts at the title's weight, then the integration's notes, then what
a click runs, then the shared rate-limit lines last. The box grows to
its widest line, up to the width of the screen, instead of cutting the
breakdown at a fixed width. See
[A statusline chip says what it counts](/docs/features/hover-help#a-statusline-chip-says-what-it-counts).

### Chips named in words, and Hide on every chip

Right-click the statusline between its chips: *Segments ▸* lists an
integration's chip by what it counts, as in *Bitbucket PRs: pull
requests, review threads, waiting on you*, not by its id. A chip's own
right-click menu now ends with *Hide*.

A chip you hid no longer takes room from the ones still shown. When
the row has no room for a chip, *Segments ▸* marks it *(no room on the
row)* and says how many chips are hidden for width, so a chip no longer
drops off the row silently. A chip that does not fit is left off whole
instead of being cut.

### Smaller changes

- Opening Bitbucket Pipelines no longer turns the Bitbucket PRs chip's
  icon into the Pipelines one.
- An integration manifest's statusline segment takes an optional
  `label`, the few words the Segments menu shows for it.

## Upgrading

Homebrew, winget and the installers move you to 0.3.5 on your next
upgrade:

```sh
brew upgrade mnml
```

```powershell
winget upgrade ChrisMcLennan.mnml
```

Or run the installer for your platform again, from
[Install](/docs/install). winget is Microsoft's community repository and
merges each version on its own schedule, so 0.3.5 can reach it some time
after the other channels.

Then open the Marketplace tab under INTEGRATIONS: an installed Jira or
Bitbucket 0.2.3 reads *update available*. The one-chip statusline needs
0.2.4 of both. See [Marketplace](/docs/integrations/marketplace#updating).
